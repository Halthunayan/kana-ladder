Scheduler

Algorithm
FSRS models memory directly. SM-2 is the classic ease-factor method.
Target retention
The chance of recall you want at the moment a card comes back. Lower means longer gaps and fewer reviews.

Daily load

New words per day
The floor for how many unseen words enter each day, not the ceiling. The deck holds 1,759 words.
Review cap per day
Upper limit on scheduled reviews, so a backlog never buries you.
Follow-up cards per day
Recall and listening cards for words you already know. Their own budget, so a new word no longer starves the word before it.
Let easy days earn more
Each new word you grade Easy on sight buys another one, up to double the daily figure. A miss gives some back.

Card direction

Reverse cards (EN → JP)
Recall in Japanese. Harder than recognition, so it starts later by default.
Keep the two directions apart
A word is never tested both ways on the same day, so its answer is never fresh from the card before.
Listening cards
A third card that plays the word and asks what you heard. 0 Japanese voice(s) found on this device.
Speech speed
Percent of normal. 85 is a clear teaching pace; real Japanese runs at 100 and above.
Play as the card appears
Reads the Japanese aloud the moment a card is shown, not only when you tap the speaker. Silent on English to Japanese cards, and a conjugation card plays the dictionary form, never the answer.
Vary the speed and voice
Jitters each playback around your setting and rotates between the device voices. Without it you learn one recording rather than the word.

The trip

Trip date
The day you land. The study screen counts down to it, and new words pause for the last ten days so what you have can settle instead of growing.

      Scheduling defaults to FSRS‑6. Each card carries a difficulty and a stability, and the next review is placed
      on the day your chance of recalling it falls to your target retention. Lapsed cards drop back into short steps
      before rejoining the long schedule. SM‑2, the classic ease‑factor method, remains selectable above.
    

    
Every scheduled answer is written to a review log held on this phone: the card, the grade, and how long it had been since you last saw it. Nothing else can reconstruct that later, and it is what fitting the scheduler to your own memory would need. The log travels inside your backups.

    
The daily figures govern the scheduled queue only. Study ahead sessions are unlimited and can be run as often as you like; they pull future reviews forward and keep introducing new words.

    
Installed as a Home Screen app, this deck runs with no network at all. The 1,000 words, the schedule and the interface are already on the phone.

    
iOS gives a Home Screen app its own storage, separate from Safari's. Study from the Home Screen icon and stay there: cards answered in a Safari tab are recorded against a different, unrelated schedule.
